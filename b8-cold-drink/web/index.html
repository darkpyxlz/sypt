    How it works

    The bath is brine, crushed ice and salt crystals. Salt dissolves and ice melts at finite rates. Heat flows between the room, the foam, the glass, the brine, the ice and the drink, and the model steps the heat content of each forward every half second, so energy is conserved (every run reports the check). The drink's heat does not reach the whole bath at once: the bath is split into rings around the can, and heat crosses from ring to ring at the rate still brine conducts it, times a convection factor for the brine moving through the ice (1 = still, 20 by default, inf = perfectly mixed). So the brine next to the can warms first and its ice melts first, as the tank picture shows. The styrofoam box is not a perfect insulator: heat from the room leaks in through its walls, its floor (the tank stands on it), its lid and the gaps around the lid, about 0.34 W/K in all, and every run says how much came in from the room and how much from the drink. The energy bookkeeping line only checks that every joule is accounted for. The can lies on its side, as in the rig, drawn to scale (it can be stood upright instead). Each wetted area follows the liquid level: the glass up to the surface, the ice below it, and the can wherever there is liquid on both sides of its wall (not the strip along its top where its gas is). The can is lowered in over 10 seconds, so the level rises smoothly as it goes under. The drink can freeze, gradually, as its sugar concentrates. The page loads Pyodide (Python compiled for the browser), copies in the model files unchanged, and runs them on your computer. Nothing is sent anywhere.

    Putting in your measurements

    Open All inputs. Each number carries a tag:

      Change the values you have measured, then Download .toml. The file runs unchanged with python3 b8_simple.py --inputs b8_inputs.toml, and Load .toml reads it back here, as does a short file holding only the lines that change. Copy link puts your changes in the address, so you can send a scenario to someone else.

      What matters most for the time to 5 °C is the heat transfer across the can: fit a drink run against a brine probe within about 5 mm of the can and enter the measured U. A second probe out in the bath measures the convection factor: the default puts the brine next to the can about 6 K above the rest. For the bath temperature, it is the ice and salt rates: a 15-minute run with no drink pins them, and tells "bulk" from "salt-limited" ice surfaces. For how fast the bath warms, it is the box's leak: start from box_test.toml, log 5 kg of cold water in the closed box for 3 hours, and move box leak, measured until the curve matches (the README gives a formula too).
